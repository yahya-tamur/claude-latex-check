For a quantity $Q$ at position $\mathbf{r}$ relative to a chosen origin:

- **Zeroth moment:** $\sum Q$, the total amount.
- **First moment:** $\sum Q\,\mathbf{r}$, the amount weighted by distance once.
- **Second moment:** $\sum Q\,r^2$ (or the tensor $\sum Q\,r_i r_j$), the amount weighted by distance squared.

For continuous distributions the sums become integrals, e.g. $\int \rho(\mathbf{r})\,\mathbf{r}\,dV$.

| Name | Formula | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Moment of force (torque)** | $\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}$ | x |
| **Angular momentum** | $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p}$ | x |
| **First moment of mass** | $\sum m_i\mathbf{r}_i = M\,\mathbf{r}_{\text{cm}}$ | x |
| **Moment of inertia** | $I = \sum m_i r_i^2$ | Resistance ($\tau = I\alpha$) |
| **Electric dipole moment** | $\mathbf{p} = \sum q_i \mathbf{r}_i$ | x |
| **Magnetic dipole moment** | $\boldsymbol{\mu} = I\mathbf{A}$ for a current loop | x |

- **They summarize a distribution.** a series in $1/r$.
